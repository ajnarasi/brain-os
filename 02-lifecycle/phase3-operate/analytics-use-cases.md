# Operate — Analytics Use Cases

The analytics use cases the Brain covers. Delivered as plain-English narratives with drill-down on click, not as a dashboard.

## Weekly business narrative
Delivered Monday morning. Structured as:
1. **Headline** — "This week, you did $X in sales (+Y% WoW)"
2. **What's working** — 2–3 positive trends with specific drivers
3. **What's worth watching** — 2–3 concerning trends with specific drivers
4. **One thing to do this week** — single actionable suggestion

## Monthly business review
Delivered on the 1st. Month-over-month + YoY compare. Organized by: revenue, approval rate, fraud, disputes, settlement, optimization opportunities.

## Incident narratives (on-demand)
Triggered by IncidentAgent. Not scheduled.

## Custom queries
Merchant can ask arbitrary questions: "Why did my Saturday sales drop?" / "Which SKUs are driving the most chargebacks?" / "What's my best-performing APM in Germany?"

## Benchmarking narratives
"Merchants like you (same size, same vertical, anonymized) have an average approval rate of X%. You're at Y%. The gap is mostly driven by [specific factor]."

## Optimization narratives
Paired with OptimizationAgent. "Here are 3 specific changes that could move your auth rate up 0.4%–0.8%." Each suggestion has expected impact + effort + risk.

## Forecasting narratives
"Based on the last 12 weeks, next week's expected revenue is $X ± $Y. Drivers: [list]."

## Why narratives and not dashboards

Nobody reads dashboards. A plain-English narrative delivered once a week gets read by the owner. The dashboard is still available for drill-down, but the narrative is the product.

## Narrative quality bar

Every narrative must:
- Cite the underlying data (merchant can click to drill down)
- Avoid generic statements ("your sales went up" is not a narrative; "your sales went up because a new product launched on Tuesday" is)
- Suggest an action or flag nothing actionable explicitly
- Respect the merchant's attention — ~150 words for weekly, ~500 for monthly
