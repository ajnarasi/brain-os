## Overview

This profile captures three publicly announced strategic merchant relationships that ground the Fiserv Brain demo in real-world enterprise payment dynamics: Yum! Brands' in-house "Byte by Yum" tech stack, the Costco Anywhere Visa by Citi co-brand refresh, and Inspire Brands' expansion of its Fiserv Carat enterprise commerce relationship. Together they cover the three archetypes Brain must reason about - a vertically integrated global QSR operator, a warehouse-club co-brand card economics story, and a multi-brand restaurant holding company running a unified commerce platform with Fiserv.

## Yum! Brands - scale and digital flywheel

**Scale.** Yum! Brands' 2024 Annual Report describes a footprint of more than 61,000 restaurants across 155+ countries and territories, spanning KFC, Taco Bell, Pizza Hut and The Habit Burger Grill. The company opened 4,535 gross new stores during 2024, reinforcing its status as one of the largest restaurant operators globally (Yum 2024 Annual Report, s2.q4cdn.com).

**Digital sales and mix.** Yum reported approximately $40 billion of system digital sales in 2024, with digital representing more than 50% of system sales - a milestone the company has highlighted as proof of the "Byte by Yum" digital flywheel working at scale (Yum 2024 Annual Report). Byte by Yum is described as an integrated SaaS ecosystem - covering POS, e-commerce, loyalty, delivery orchestration, AI-driven operations and analytics - which Yum extends to franchisees and is beginning to position as a commercial product to the broader restaurant industry (restaurantdive.com).

**Dragontail and AI.** Following the 2021 Dragontail acquisition, Yum has integrated kitchen order management and AI-based store operations into the Byte platform, and has publicly highlighted SuperApp experiences that fold loyalty, ordering and delivery into a single interface. Yum's own materials describe Byte as a competitive moat in the franchised QSR segment (Yum 2024 Annual Report; restaurantdive.com).

**Q1 2025 performance.** Yum's Q1 2025 earnings release (April 30, 2025) reported worldwide system sales growth of approximately 5%, with Taco Bell US same-store sales up roughly 9%, KFC worldwide same-store sales up about 5%, and Pizza Hut down around 3%. Digital sales reached approximately $9 billion, representing a digital mix of about 55%. The company opened 751 gross new units in the quarter (Yum Q1 2025 earnings release, s2.q4cdn.com; cnbc.com; stocktitan.net).

**Relevance to Brain personas.** Yum is the reference point for the synthetic **"NorthGate QSR"** persona: a multi-brand franchise operator running an owned stack, obsessed with digital mix, store-count expansion and kitchen/operations AI. NorthGate's pressure points (franchisee onboarding speed, digital authorization rates across 150+ countries, loyalty-to-tender attach) can be grounded in Byte's publicly disclosed playbook.

## Costco Anywhere Visa by Citi - co-brand economics

**Product positioning.** The Costco Anywhere Visa by Citi is Costco's sole co-brand credit card in the US and doubles as a Costco membership card. Its public benefits page (citi.com, costco.com/credit-card.html) positions the card as a high-value rewards product for warehouse-club members and commuters.

**January 2025 refresh.** Public coverage in January 2025 confirmed a meaningful update to the card's rewards structure, which took effect in early 2025 (foxbusiness.com; bgr.com). The post-refresh earn rates, as disclosed on citi.com and costco.com/credit-card.html, are:

| Category | Earn rate |
|----------|-----------|
| Gas and EV charging at Costco | 5% (previously 4%) |
| Other eligible gas and EV charging (non-Costco) | 4% |
| Restaurants and eligible travel (including Costco Travel) | 3% |
| All other purchases at Costco and costco.com | 2% |
| All other purchases | 1% |

**Gas cap.** The bonus gas earn rate is subject to a $7,000 annual combined purchase cap on gas and EV charging, after which qualifying spend earns 1%. At the 5% rate that implies a maximum gas bonus of $350 per cardholder-year, a number widely cited by consumer press covering the 2025 update (foxbusiness.com; bgr.com).

**Why it matters for Brain.** The Costco Visa is a canonical example of how a single co-brand product re-engineers merchant economics - interchange, loyalty funding, fuel rewards caps, and membership tie-in all live in one artifact. For Brain's "portfolio-level risk and revenue" demos, the public card refresh is useful as a real anchor: it shows how an issuer-acquirer-merchant triangle publishes changes, and how rewards caps intersect with category MCCs.

## Inspire Brands and Fiserv Carat - unified commerce at scale

**Announcement.** On September 27, 2023, Inspire Brands announced an expanded relationship with Fiserv's Carat enterprise commerce platform, extending an existing Dunkin' engagement to cover additional Inspire concepts, explicitly including Sonic and Baskin-Robbins (pymnts.com; newsroom.fiserv.com; qsrmagazine.com; businesswire.com; verdictfoodservice.com).

**Scope of the partnership.** Per the Fiserv newsroom release and PYMNTS coverage, the expanded relationship is designed to unify in-store, in-app and online commerce across Inspire's brands, covering:

- Card acceptance and authorization
- Settlement and funding
- Reconciliation and reporting
- Gift card issuance and redemption across brands
- Omnichannel fraud and data services

**Executive commentary.** Public coverage quotes Inspire Brands CFO Kate Jaspon on the value of a single commerce backbone across Dunkin', Sonic and Baskin-Robbins, and Casey Klyszeiko, head of Global eCommerce and Fiserv Carat, on enabling a unified digital commerce experience across Inspire's portfolio (newsroom.fiserv.com; pymnts.com; qsrmagazine.com).

**Why it matters for Brain.** This is the clearest public anchor for the Brain persona **"Indigo Road Holdings"** - a multi-brand restaurant group consolidating payment stacks into Fiserv Carat. Indigo Road's questions in the demo (where does a failed Sonic mobile-order auth show up vs. a Dunkin' in-store chip read, and what does the cross-brand gift card posting look like in Carat's reporting layer) map directly to the publicly described capabilities of the Inspire Brands deal. The press release also legitimizes Brain's "one operator, many brands, one settlement stream" scenario without needing to disclose any confidential Fiserv relationship detail.

## Cross-cutting themes for the Brain demo

1. **Owned stacks vs. platform partnerships.** Yum has built Byte internally while Inspire has deliberately leaned into Fiserv Carat. Brain should be able to reason about both patterns when answering "how does this merchant actually run?"
2. **Digital mix as an economic driver.** Yum's public >50% digital mix and Inspire's unified commerce narrative both point to the same truth: once digital clears half of sales, the payment stack becomes a revenue system, not a back office.
3. **Co-brand cards as a second P&L.** The Costco Visa refresh is a reminder that issuer economics - caps, categories, MCCs - sit on top of merchant acceptance and can change mid-year with real consumer impact.
4. **Persona grounding.** NorthGate QSR (Yum-style), Indigo Road (Inspire-style) and Casa Rosa Taqueria (Clover-scale SMB, covered in the companion file) cover the three scales Brain must operate across: global operator, multi-brand enterprise, and single-location SMB.
